# SAR 2026 Framework - HA 6

Use this reference for structure and interpretation. Verify current official HAI materials when the requested work may be affected by a later version.

## Applicability and interpretation rules

HA Standard 6 is effective for accreditation from 1 October 2026. Before applying it to a live case, confirm the assessment date and any HAI transition instruction.

The standard distinguishes two directive verbs:

- **ensure / ทำให้มั่นใจว่า:** establish a systematic, effective service or safety process and continuously improve it;
- **assure / ทำให้เชื่อมั่นว่า:** design and manage a system affected by external or less-controllable factors so predicted performance remains acceptable and is continuously improved.

Roman-numbered notes `(i), (ii), (iii)` are contextual implementation support and may not all apply. They are not separate scoring points.

## Purpose of SAR

SAR serves three purposes:

1. organizational learning from success, failure, completeness, and cross-team review;
2. communication with surveyors;
3. a durable basis for continued improvement and spread.

It is not an activity inventory or a promotional narrative.

## Current tool family

The 2026 self-assessment package used with HA Standard 6 includes:

- Hospital Profile 2026;
- SAR Parts I-IV;
- CLT/PCT Profile and important disease/procedure results;
- ENV Checklist as a separate self-assessment aid whose lessons can inform II-3.

Parts I-III are assessed at chapter/subchapter level, such as I-1.1, rather than by assigning separate scores to every numbered implementation sub-item. Sub-items remain useful as design and completeness prompts.

## Parts I-III: four required components

### 1. Context

Summarize only material current conditions, problems, risks, strengths, constraints, and opportunities that affect system design, implementation, or success. Context must explain why the organization designed this system and selected these priorities.

Avoid copying the Hospital Profile or writing generic hospital history.

### 2. Development already undertaken

Use 3P:

- **Purpose:** intended value or result aligned with the overall requirement and local context.
- **Process:** important design and implementation choices, including target groups, coverage, roles, controls, communication, and monitoring.
- **Performance:** outputs only when necessary, then meaningful outcomes and evidence of effectiveness.

Include learning and improvement when they demonstrate maturity. Do not list every committee meeting or policy.

### 3. Continued development plan

State material development work for the next 1-2 years. Each plan should arise from a documented gap, risk, poor/variable result, incomplete coverage, or next maturity step. A plan is not “continue to operate.” State what will change, why, intended result, and how progress will be known.

### 4. Results

Select measures that reflect the chapter's overall requirement and important local context. Prefer outcomes. Use process measures only when they represent a critical control or explain the causal pathway to outcomes.

## 3C-DALI and 3P

3C identifies the basis for the improvement aim:

- **Concepts:** sound principles and professional knowledge;
- **Context:** the organization's actual challenge and existing system;
- **Criteria:** the intent and requirements of the standard.

DALI expresses maturity:

- **Design:** purposeful system design;
- **Action:** reliable implementation in important areas;
- **Learning:** monitoring, analysis, reflection, and learning;
- **Improve:** changes based on learning, spread, and integration.

3P is a compact writing grammar. 3C-DALI is the reasoning and maturity model behind it. A complete paragraph may contain both without naming either framework.

## Part IV

Part IV presents organization-level performance results across:

1. IV-1 health care/clinical and health-promotion results;
2. IV-2 patient and other customer focus;
3. IV-3 workforce;
4. IV-4 leadership and governance;
5. IV-5 important work systems;
6. IV-6 financial results.

For important indicators, show:

- operational definition when ambiguity exists;
- target and current performance;
- multi-year trend;
- comparison/benchmark when meaningful;
- run chart or control chart when it improves interpretation;
- annotations for interventions or context changes;
- analysis of variation, causal interpretation limits, and resulting PDSA/PDCA.

## Nine essential safety areas in the official SAR 2026 form

Use the current official form for exact indicator definitions. The 2026 structure covers:

1. wrong patient/side/site/procedure surgery;
2. important infections, including SSI, VAP, CAUTI, and CLABSI as applicable;
3. occupational infection among staff;
4. medication error and adverse drug events;
5. wrong blood/patient/group/type;
6. patient identification error;
7. diagnostic error;
8. laboratory/pathology analysis or reporting error;
9. emergency department triage error, including under-triage and over-triage.

For harmful events at level E or above, the form emphasizes review/root-cause analysis and evidence that preventive controls were implemented.

## VRGDISK

Use these as optional prompts:

- Value: what patients or other recipients gained;
- Risk: material risk and control design;
- Good practice: effective practice, innovation, or transferable learning;
- Diversity: adaptation to different patients, settings, or staff;
- Integration: connections across professions, units, and systems;
- Spirituality: humane and spiritual dimensions relevant to care or work;
- Knowledge: knowledge created and reused.

Do not force all seven into every chapter.
