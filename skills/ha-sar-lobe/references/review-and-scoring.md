# Review and HA Scoring Calibration

## Scoring principle

Score the chapter's overall requirement. HA Standard 6 defines 54 process chapters/subchapters in Parts I-III and 6 result categories in Part IV. Use detailed sub-items as explanatory practice guidance to test completeness and implementation, not as independent points to add or subtract. Give whole-number scores. When the model reviews a hospital SAR, label its working score **AI-Assisted Score**, not Surveyor Score. It is advisory and remains evidence-dependent unless relevant sources have been triangulated and the survey team has made its judgment.

The official Scoring Guideline is a maturity tool aligned with 3P-DALI. Its purpose is continuous quality improvement and sustainable development, not merely reaching a certification threshold. Safety-essential criteria announced by HAI must be implemented concretely and must be covered even at partial-implementation level.

## Maturity anchors

| Score | Process anchor | Result anchor |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Design and early implementation | Measurement has begun |
| 2 | Partial implementation of important requirements | Relevant and valid measures are tracked |
| 3 | Effective implementation across important areas/issues, aligned with the standard and local context, with demonstrated effect of implementation | Measures cover most important issues and are analyzed and used for continuing development |
| 4 | Continuous improvement of important processes and integration with related systems | Most important results are better than a comparable average or show a continuously improving trend |
| 5 | Role-model/good practice, knowledge capture, innovation, and demonstrable impact | Important results are in the top quartile |

Do not average isolated fragments mechanically. Use the lowest material maturity constraint: strong results cannot fully compensate for an unreliable process, and a sophisticated process cannot sustain a high score without results.

Do not confuse `effective implementation` at score 3 with the final outcome indicator. It means the designed practice is implemented across important areas and achieves the intended operational effect under process control. Final outcome maturity is assessed separately in Part IV.

Do not reduce a score automatically because a detail or supporting document is absent from the SAR narrative. Put it under **For Finding** as evidence to request. Treat it as a material gap only when the hospital states that the system is absent or incomplete, reliable evidence contradicts the claim, or the omission materially prevents the overall requirement from being effective. Likewise, a result below target does not by itself force score 2 when implementation is effective and the organization analyzes and uses the measure; good and sustained comparative results become decisive at scores 4-5.

When team members disagree on a score, record each view's strengths and opportunities for improvement, improve the system, and reassess. The SAR should state what is done well and the development plan; verification evidence should remain organized at the hospital.

## Score review questions

1. Is the purpose aligned with the overall requirement and local context?
2. Is the design systematic or a collection of activities?
3. Is implementation reliable in all important areas and populations?
4. Are measures valid, timely, and actually used for decisions?
5. Are there documented learning cycles and specific system changes?
6. Is integration visible across professions, units, leadership, and support systems?
7. Are results sustained, meaningful, and interpreted against target or comparison?
8. Would the claims survive document review, interview, observation, tracer, and raw-data review?

## Confidence

Report confidence separately from score:

- **High:** triangulated current evidence supports process, coverage, learning, and results.
- **Moderate:** coherent evidence exists but one important source or period is missing.
- **Low:** score relies mainly on narrative, old documents, unverified interviews, or unsupported claims.

Confidence is optional in the user-facing table. Do not let a low-confidence narrative masquerade as a definitive surveyor judgment; use precise For Finding requests instead.

## Gap-to-next-level output

State no more than the few material gaps that constrain the score. Phrase them as evidence or system needs, for example:

- verify implementation coverage in night/weekend shifts;
- demonstrate two completed learning cycles rather than one isolated project;
- connect unit-level practices into an organization-wide control;
- add sustained outcome trend and meaningful comparison;
- show that results were used to change design or resource allocation.

Do not recommend creating paperwork solely to increase a score.

## Common failure modes

- copying standard language as self-assessment;
- listing committees, meetings, policies, or training without implementation evidence;
- calling counts of activities “outcomes”;
- reporting a percentage without numerator, denominator, period, or definition;
- selecting only favorable results and hiding variation;
- claiming integration from coordination alone;
- claiming innovation without evidence of novelty, adoption, or impact;
- giving score 4 or 5 without sustained results and mature learning cycles;
- using a future plan to justify the current score;
- mixing hospital-specific evidence from different organizations.

## Part IV quality test

An indicator is strong when it is high-impact, actionable, reliable, interpretable, connected to an improved process, and useful for learning. Prefer outcomes over routine process compliance. Use process measures when they are critical controls or necessary explanatory measures.

For safety events, do not interpret a lower report count as safer without examining reporting culture, detection, denominator, severity mix, and review completeness.
