# SPA 2022 — Implementation and SAR Guidance Index

Use this reference when answering “มาตรฐานข้อนี้ควรทำอะไร,” building an evidence request, or drafting/reviewing SAR Parts I-III. SPA means Standards-Practice-Assessment and is designed as an empowerment-evaluation aid: it makes implementation more concrete and ends with self-assessment prompts.

The three source books were written for **HA Standard 5**, not HA Standard 6. They remain valuable implementation guidance, but the applicable HA Standard 6 overall requirement and SAR 2026 form control the final interpretation and submission structure.

## Operating rule

Use this sequence:

`Applicable HA6 overall requirement -> edition 6-to-5 topic mapping -> SPA practice prompts -> SPA assessment prompts -> hospital evidence -> SAR 2026 synthesis`

Do not reverse the sequence. SPA cannot create a mandatory requirement absent from the applicable standard, and the activity list is not evidence that the hospital performs the work.

Before using a chapter in detail:

1. Read the HA Standard 6 overall requirement and important changes.
2. Map to SPA by topic, not code alone.
3. Open the SPA source pages listed below; read `กิจกรรมที่ควรดำเนินการ` and `ประเด็นที่ควรพิจารณา/ตอบในการประเมินตนเอง`.
4. Select only prompts relevant to the hospital's context, risk, service scope, and maturity.
5. Convert prompts into an evidence map; verify design, actual action/coverage, learning, improvement, integration, and result separately.
6. Draft in the current SAR 2026 structure. Do not paste SPA bullets or theoretical explanations into the SAR.

## Durable SPA principles

- Standards become useful when applied in daily work: “คุยกันเล่น - เห็นของจริง - อิงการวิจัย.”
- A systematic approach specifies steps, accountable roles, and a clear cycle; it is repeatable, measured, evaluated, and improved.
- The suggested practices are options for leaders and teams to choose according to context. They are not a universal mandatory checklist.
- A credible self-assessment requires the standard to have been implemented for some time.
- The writing emphasis is the **lesson and result of implementation**, especially context and Study/Learning in PDSA, rather than a long explanation of methods or theory.
- The assessment tables separate **practice** from **result**. Keep this distinction when requesting evidence and writing SAR.
- For organizations pursuing Advanced HA, SPA offers broader performance-oriented prompts, but these still require contextual judgment and evidence.

## Rapid Assessment for SAR evidence

SPA Part III recommends rapid assessment to answer a decision-relevant question with the smallest practical data burden. The method may use observation, interview, record review, or a small sample; SPA gives 10-30 cases as an example, not a universal statistical standard.

Use it when an important SAR claim lacks direct evidence:

1. Define the standard-linked concern and the decision the result will inform.
2. Form a narrow question.
3. Select the least burdensome method that can answer it with acceptable bias control.
4. Examine several relevant points/settings when variation matters.
5. Use the result to identify a development opportunity, change the process, or support a carefully bounded SAR statement.

Label sample size, period, method, limitations, and whether the result is representative. Never generalize a small convenience sample beyond what it supports.

## Clinical Self Enquiry for Part III

Clinical Self Enquiry integrates evidence across the patient journey. Useful sources include:

- bedside review and patient/family experience;
- clinical tracer;
- adverse-event review and trigger-based medical-record review;
- tracing patient-safety goals/guides;
- medical-record review against standards and quality of care;
- other routine quality-review activities and meaningful stories;
- proxy diseases that reveal quality at a specific care step.

Use the patient-care process as the organizing spine. For SAR, combine:

1. a concise statement of the general system and its coverage;
2. selected disease/procedure examples showing how the system works;
3. clinical quality summaries or tracers for end-to-end depth where useful;
4. strengths, adverse events, gaps, learning, improvement, and results from the same evidence chain.

Avoid repeating a full clinical tracer inside every standard response. Cross-reference it when it carries the detail.

## Evidence request generated from SPA

For each chapter, translate SPA into this compact request:

| Lens | Ask for |
|---|---|
| Context | Important population, service scope, risk, constraint, and performance gap |
| Purpose | Intended patient/customer/workforce/organizational value |
| Design | Workflow, roles, criteria, controls, escalation, coordination, and resources |
| Action | Actual coverage by unit, profession, shift, population, and period |
| Learning | Audit, tracer, interview, observation, rapid assessment, event/record review, and lesson |
| Improve | Specific changes, repeat cycles, spread, and integration |
| Result | Valid measures, trends, comparison, variation, unintended effects, and interpretation |

The final SAR should communicate the causal chain, not reproduce this table mechanically.
