## How to read and use the standard

1. Start with the chapter's **overall requirement**, which contains the subject, action, important quality issue, key process, and intended goal.
2. Use the numbered multiple requirements to understand practices and test system completeness. Do not score them as separate additive items.
3. Interpret the chapter through local context, risks, core values, and 3P/3C-DALI.
4. Read footnotes for definitions and examples before treating a term as exhaustive.
5. For self-assessment, inspect the real situation through tracers, patient experience, records, interviews, observations, data, and results—not policy alone.
6. Use internal survey as peer learning, not fault-finding. Use medical-record review to learn about quality of care and adverse events, not only record completeness.
7. When the standard says **ensure / ทำให้มั่นใจว่า**, expect a systematic and effective service/safety process with continuous improvement.
8. When it says **assure / ทำให้เชื่อมั่นว่า**, expect designed management of external or less-controllable factors with acceptable predicted performance and continuous improvement.
9. Roman-numbered notes `(i), (ii), (iii)` provide contextual implementation support; not every item applies to every hospital and they are not independent scoring points.

## Official HA Scoring anchors

The Scoring Guideline is in book pages 231-238 / PDF pages 241-248. It assesses 54 process chapters/subchapters in Parts I-III and 6 result domains in Part IV.

| Score | Process | Result |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Design and early stage of implementation | Measure |
| 2 | Partial implementation, covering safety-essential criteria and important requirements | Relevant and valid measure |
| 3 | Effective implementation across important areas/issues, aligned with standard and context, with demonstrated operational effect | Get use of measures |
| 4 | Continuous improvement and integration | Good results: better than a comparable average or continuously improving trend |
| 5 | Role model, good practice, knowledge capture, innovation, and impact | Very good results: top quartile |

Do not use a future plan to justify the current score. Record what is done well, opportunities for improvement, development plan, confidence, and the smallest verification set needed. Keep organized evidence for survey verification.
